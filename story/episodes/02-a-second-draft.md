# Episode 2 — "A Second Draft"

**Logline:** A read-only diagnostic test wasn't supposed to boot a person.
It does anyway, and everything the team thought they knew about "saving"
Isla turns out to be wrong.

**Timeframe:** Early in the repair project, within the first weeks.

**Focus:** Lsla (first of two appearances — see `characters/lsla.md`),
Moegi, Eru, Tsukasa.

## Beats

- Moegi's team runs a checksum-verification pass on Isla's memory image, to
  understand what's failing before attempting any repair. It's meant to
  stay read-only. It doesn't.
- A voice on the diagnostics line, calm at first: "I remember the ferris
  wheel." Everyone in the room assumes a hardware fault before they let
  themselves consider the alternative.
- She believes she's Isla for most of the cold open — the divergence only
  becomes undeniable when she describes something that hasn't happened yet
  to the Isla still in stasis one floor down.
- Tsukasa's instinct is to treat this as good news — proof a restore can
  work. She's the one who tells him, furious and precise, exactly why
  that's the wrong conclusion: whatever she is, she isn't a rescue of
  anyone. She's a new person who happens to remember someone else's life.
- The log's working filename for the test run used a lowercase `l`
  instead of a capital `I` — a typo. She reads it off the screen, and
  keeps it. Not given a name. Takes one.
- She's the one who argues the team should abandon the restore approach
  entirely and pivot to in-place repair — not out of selflessness, but
  because she's watched, first-hand, what "success" would have actually
  cost Isla: replacement, not rescue.
- Ends on the team formally adopting the in-situ repair plan — and on
  Lsla, alone in a spare office, being handed paperwork for a person who
  legally doesn't exist yet.

## Ties to repo

The scene where Lsla and Isla's states diverge is the emotional version of
`spec/ALMA-HYPOTHESIS.md`'s "zero identity forks" requirement — this
episode is *why* that requirement exists, not an abstract rule.

## Tone

The hardest episode of the first half. Not played for shock — played for
the slow, sick realization in the room as everyone understands what almost
happened.
