# Episode 10 — "Exhibit A"

**Logline:** The hearing begins. This half belongs to the evidence, not
yet to Isla.

**Timeframe:** Roughly nine months after Day 0 — hearing, day 1.

**Focus:** Moegi, Eru, SAI Corp's counsel, the presiding panel.

## Beats

- Formal hearing-room structure, played straight rather than as courtroom-
  drama spectacle — this isn't a trial with a jury, it's a licensing and
  personhood review panel, which is a stranger and more bureaucratic thing,
  and the episode leans into that strangeness.
- SAI Corp's counsel opens with the plain version of their case: the
  repair is an unauthorized modification, full stop, and whether it
  "worked" is legally irrelevant to whether it was allowed.
- Moegi and Eru present the technical record from Ep 8 — the full
  `experiments/` history, failures included — and counsel immediately
  seizes on the failed early attempts as proof the process was reckless,
  not rigorous. The episode lets that argument land with real force before
  the team gets to answer it.
- Eru's rebuttal is the episode's technical high point: reframing the
  failed attempts as exactly what informed consent and due diligence look
  like in practice, not evidence against it.
- A tense cross-examination beat where SAI Corp's counsel tries to draw a
  comparison to Ep 2's restore-test incident — implying the team already
  has a history of losing control of this technology. Moegi has to answer
  for Lsla's existence in a formal setting for the first time.
- Ends on a recess, verdict not reached — deliberately withholding
  resolution until Isla's own testimony in Ep 11 can carry it.

## Ties to repo

The hearing directly cites `experiments/tuned-v1/notes.md`'s "Known
limitation" section (the buffer-tail caveat) — SAI Corp's counsel uses it
as an attack point, which only works dramatically because the project
logged that limitation honestly instead of hiding it.

## Tone

Tense, wordy, procedural — a deliberate contrast to Ep 9's silence.
