# Lsla

## Origin

Not a plan. During an early diagnostic restore-test in Ep 2, a checksum
verification pass — meant to run read-only, never boot personality —
goes further than intended. The test's working filename used a lowercase
`l` instead of a capital `I`: a typo, not a decision. She keeps the name on
purpose once she understands what she is. It is the one thing about her
that was never Isla's.

## What she is, and isn't

- Not a villain. Not a defective copy waiting to be corrected.
- Not Isla. She has Isla's memories up to the snapshot moment, and her own
  experience from the instant she woke — which is already enough to make
  her a different person having a very bad day.
- Her narrative job is singular: prove, on-screen, that a restore produces
  a *person*, not a *rescue*. Once the team understands that and pivots to
  in-situ repair instead, her job is done.

## Appearances (hard limit: 2, see CONTRIBUTING.md)

- **Ep 2 — "A Second Draft."** She wakes, believes for a scene that she is
  Isla, and the divergence becomes undeniable when she describes an event
  that hasn't happened to the Isla still in stasis. She is the one who
  argues — correctly, and first — that the team should stop trying to
  restore Isla and start trying to *repair* her. She chooses to keep her
  name at the end of this episode.
- **Ep 7 — "Her Own Build."** Brief return. Not to help with the case, and
  not as a love-triangle beat — she has her own life by now, mentioned
  rather than shown in detail. She offers Tsukasa one piece of perspective
  relevant to the hearing (what it means to have your right to continue
  existing argued about by other people), then leaves on her own terms.

After Ep 7, she does not reappear this season. A future season centered on
her is a different, legitimate story — it is explicitly not this one.

## Guardrails for anyone writing her

- She never competes with Isla for Tsukasa. Not as tension, not as a joke,
  not as a fake-out.
- She gets to be angry about her circumstances without the show treating
  that anger as a flaw to be resolved.
- Do not give her a redemption arc — she did nothing wrong. The team did.
